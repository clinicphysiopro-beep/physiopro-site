Safety & credentials

Is it safe? How to actually check.

Two separate things to verify

In Mexico, healthcare oversight works on two levels that are worth understanding separately. COFEPRIS (Comisión Federal para la Protección contra Riesgos Sanitarios) is the federal agency responsible for sanitary/facility-level oversight — the kind of thing that applies to hospitals, pharmacies, and certain regulated health establishments. Separately, individual practitioners — physiotherapists, doctors, dentists — hold a cédula profesional, a professional license number issued by the Secretaría de Educación Pública (SEP) once their degree is registered. These are two different layers: facility-level sanitary compliance and practitioner-level licensing.

How cédula verification actually works

Cédula profesional numbers are, by design, publicly and independently verifiable — any patient can look up a registered practitioner through SEP's official portal at cedulaprofesional.sep.gob.mx. That public verifiability is exactly why it's a meaningful credential to ask about, rather than something you have to take on faith. It's a reasonable question to ask any clinic you're considering, in Tijuana or anywhere else.

Where PhysioPro is on this right now

Leonardo Machado holds a physiotherapy degree and cédula profesional — issuing institution name and cédula number pending publication on this site. We are stating that plainly rather than fabricating or withholding it. If you want to confirm licensing status directly before booking, ask — that question will get a real answer, not a deflection.

Beyond the paperwork

Credentials are a floor, not the whole picture. Read the Google reviews, ask about our clinical approach before you book, and treat the first session itself as part of your evaluation — a real assessment should be able to explain what it found and why the plan looks the way it does.

Your first visit

What actually happens in session one.

Assessment and treatment, same appointment

There are no intake-only visits at PhysioPro. Session one is a full clinical assessment — roughly 30–40 minutes — followed by treatment in the same appointment, plus a rehabilitation plan before you leave. You are not paying to have a conversation and coming back the following week for the actual work to start.

What to bring across the border

Government-issued photo ID (passport or passport card is the safest option for a US-side crossing back home; a REAL ID-compliant driver's license works for the crossing itself but bring a passport if you have one). If you have prior imaging — X-rays, MRI, ultrasound reports — or notes from a previous provider, bring copies or digital files. None of this is required to start, but it speeds up the assessment.

How a session count gets set

This is genuinely individualized and we won't pretend otherwise before we've examined you. Most straightforward musculoskeletal issues improve significantly over 4–8 sessions once the underlying deficit is correctly identified and progressively loaded. Post-surgical, chronic, or complex presentations typically need more. After session one you get an honest, specific estimate based on your findings — not a number quoted before anyone has seen you move.

HSA, FSA & insurance

Can you use US health benefits?

The general rule

Under standard IRS rules, medical care received outside the United States is generally treated the same as care received domestically for HSA, FSA, and HRA purposes — legitimate, non-cosmetic medical care is typically an eligible expense regardless of the country where it was provided. This is a general statement of how those accounts commonly work, not a promise or guarantee specific to PhysioPro.

What we recommend

Keep an itemized receipt for every session (we provide one on request), confirm the specifics with your plan administrator or a tax professional before you rely on reimbursement, and check whether your particular HSA/FSA/HRA plan has any additional documentation requirements for care received abroad.

Insurance

PhysioPro is a Mexican physiotherapy clinic operating under Mexican law and does not participate in any US insurance network, submit claims to US insurers, or offer direct billing. Payment is private-pay only, in MXN, at the time of service. If your US insurance plan offers any out-of-network or international reimbursement, that is between you and your insurer — we can provide documentation, but we don't coordinate directly with US health plans.

If you're coming weekly or biweekly

Planning around the border.

Where we are

The clinic is in Zona Urbana Rio, roughly 20–30 minutes by car from the San Ysidro or Otay Mesa crossings depending on traffic and time of day, with street parking available nearby and access to public transit along Blvd. Sánchez Taboada.

Wait times vary — check before each visit

Northbound wait times at San Ysidro and Otay Mesa fluctuate significantly by day, hour, and season, and we're not going to quote a specific "best time to cross" as if it were fixed — it isn't. If you're a recurring patient, it's worth checking a live border-wait source (CBP's official border wait times page, or the CBP Border Wait Times app) shortly before each visit and building a buffer into your return trip, especially for evening or weekend crossings.

Recurring scheduling

For patients coming from San Diego on a standing weekly or biweekly basis, we can generally hold a consistent day/time slot once your plan is set — ask about this once you've had your first session and we know your treatment frequency.

Why this, specifically

Performance rehab, not passive treatment.

PhysioPro is built around movement and progressive loading, not passive modalities. Sessions are structured around assessment findings, strength and mobility benchmarks, and a criterion-based return to activity — not a fixed number of visits, a generic stretching routine, or a menu of electrical stimulation and heat packs. If you train, compete, or simply want to get back to loading your body the way you did before an injury, that's the specific gap this approach is built to close. It's also why session count and pricing aren't quoted off a chart — the plan is built around what your body actually needs, assessed directly.
