Documented rehabilitation outcomes.

The format

What each case study documents.

01

Initial problem

The presenting complaint in plain language — what the patient could not do, how long the problem had been present, and what had already been tried.

02

Assessment findings

What the clinical examination revealed — specific deficits in strength, load tolerance, movement quality, or tissue behavior. What the imaging showed (if any) and how it related to the clinical picture.

03

Treatment plan

The rehabilitation phases — what was treated, in what order, and why. The loading approach. The home program. The clinical decisions behind the structure.

04

Progression

Key milestones across the rehabilitation course. Where the program was adjusted and what prompted the adjustment. Setbacks documented where they occurred.

05

Outcome

Quantified where possible — pain scores, strength measures, limb symmetry index, functional test results. Before-and-after comparison against the intake findings.

06

Return to activity

What the patient returned to and at what level — specific training load, competition, occupational demand, or sport. Timeline from intake to return.

07

Key lesson

The transferable clinical insight the case illustrates — what this case demonstrates that applies to other patients with similar presentations.

A full rehabilitation course following ACL reconstruction — from early post-surgical phase through criteria-based return-to-sport clearance. Documents limb symmetry progression, hop test results, landing mechanics under fatigue, and the specific decisions that determined clearance timing.

Running — In development

Recurring Running Injury: Load Deficit Resolved

A runner with a recurring lower limb injury — the same tissue failing under the same conditions across multiple training cycles. Documents the capacity deficit identified on assessment, the progressive loading program, and the return to full training mileage without recurrence.

Post-Surgical — In development

Post-Surgical Rehabilitation: Full Activity Return

Rehabilitation following orthopaedic surgery — from the early protected phase through progressive loading to functional discharge. Documents protocol compliance, milestone achievement, and the criteria used to advance through each phase.

Back Pain — In development

Chronic Back Pain: Active Patient Returns to Full Load

A patient with recurring back pain who had tried passive treatment multiple times without lasting result. Documents what the assessment found that prior treatment had not addressed, the loading progression, and the discharge state compared to intake.

A BJJ or MMA athlete managing a significant injury with a fixed competition date. Documents the triage decisions, what was modified and what continued, how the tissue was protected under contact demands, and the outcome at competition and beyond.

A chronic tendon complaint — patellar, Achilles, or rotator cuff — that had persisted through rest and passive treatment. Documents why the tissue did not respond to prior management, the isometric and progressive loading program used, and the return to unrestricted activity.
